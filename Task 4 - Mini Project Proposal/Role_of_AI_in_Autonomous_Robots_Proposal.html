TASK 4: MINI PROJECT PROPOSAL

The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Next-Generation Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs)

Architecture, Deep Reinforcement Learning, and Embedded Edge Computing for Industrial Logistics
Author: CodeAlpha Robotics & Automation Intern
Domain: Artificial Intelligence & Autonomous Robotics
Target Platform: NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin & ROS 2 Humble
Submission Date: September 2026
Executive Summary — The transition to Industry 5.0 demands cyber-physical material transport agents capable of operating safely and deterministically in human-shared, unstructured industrial facilities. This technical engineering proposal details the complete system architecture for an intelligent Autonomous Mobile Robot (AMR). By synthesizing heterogeneous multi-sensor perception (3D LiDAR, Stereo Depth Vision, IMU), Edge-AI computing via NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, real-time semantic scene classification, and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for dynamic collision avoidance, the proposed platform delivers Level 4 industrial autonomy adhering to ISO 3691-4 safety protocols.

1. Problem Statement & Industrial Motivation

Modern smart manufacturing facilities suffer from severe material handling bottlenecks. Manual forklift operations cause over 34,900 serious injuries annually in North America alone, while traditional Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) remain constrained by expensive, fixed floor infrastructure (magnetic tapes, inductive wires, optical barcodes). When an AGV encounters an unexpected obstacle, it halts permanently, creating cascading throughput failures. Integrating modern Edge Artificial Intelligence directly onto mobile robots provides continuous semantic perception, dynamic obstacle evasion, and infrastructure-free autonomous navigation.

2. Proposed System Architecture

The proposed AMR is architected as a modular, three-tier hierarchical system integrated via ROS 2 Humble:

3. Algorithmic Framework: Edge AI & Deep Reinforcement Learning

3.1 Edge AI Semantic Perception

To operate safely around human personnel and industrial equipment, visual feeds from the stereo camera are fed into an optimized YOLOv10 model executed via NVIDIA TensorRT in FP16 precision. The model detects and tracks dynamic agents (personnel, forklifts, pallets) with sub-15ms inference latency, publishing 3D bounding obstacles directly into the local costmap.

3.2 Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance

Unlike classical local trajectory planners (such as DWA or TEB) which suffer from goal freezes in crowded corridors, the proposed robot employs a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) reinforcement learning policy trained within NVIDIA Isaac Sim:

  • State Vector ($s_t$): Subsampled 2D laser scan range array, current linear/angular velocity $(v_t, \omega_t)$, relative goal distance and orientation $(d_{\text{goal}}, \theta_{\text{goal}})$, and obstacle velocity vectors.
  • Action Space ($a_t$): Continuous linear and angular velocities $[\Delta v, \Delta \omega]$ constrained to chassis acceleration limits ($a_{\text{max}} = 1.5\text{ m/s}^2$).
  • Reward Function: Penalizes collisions (-200), penalizes excessive jerk/jostle, and rewards target distance reduction (+100 on goal arrival).

5. Industrial Safety Standards & Fail-Safe Protocol

To ensure safe coexistence with human operators, the robot implements multi-tier safety architecture compliant with ISO 3691-4:2023 and ANSI/RIA R15.08-1-2020:

  1. Hardware Safety LiDAR Interlock: SICK safety scanners monitor concentric deceleration and emergency stop fields. Physical entry into the emergency envelope directly interrupts power to motor inverters via Safe Torque Off (STO).
  2. Software Heartbeat Watchdog: The low-level STM32 microcontroller exchanges high-frequency (100 Hz) heartbeats with the Jetson AGX Orin. If edge software hangs, mechanical disk brakes engage within 20 ms.
